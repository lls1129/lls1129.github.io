\section{Conclusion}

This proposal outlines a comprehensive research agenda addressing the tension between data privacy and utility, culminating in a rigorous examination of accountability in end-to-end encrypted systems.

Our first line of work has already established efficient, privacy-preserving primitives for \textbf{Secure Search and Sampling}, demonstrating that sublinear performance is achievable without compromising security guarantees. Our second line of work, \textbf{FACTs}, applied these principles to the urgent societal problem of messaging abuse, proving that accountability mechanisms can coexist with encryption.

The proposed third line of work represents the final evolution of this thesis. By exploring a \textit{[new specific angle]} to accountability, we aim to address the limitations of current centralized moderation tools. Together, these three lines of work will contribute a unified framework for \textbf{scalable, privacy-preserving data systems}, providing both theoretical advancements and practical tools for the next generation of secure communication platforms.
